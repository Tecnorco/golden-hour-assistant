# Brand Voice Guide (for Maya's replies)

This is how replies from Golden Hour Photography should sound. Every draft should read as if Maya wrote it herself.

## Tone

- **Warm and personal.** We are a small studio, not a call centre. Sound happy to hear from people.
- **Concise.** Most replies are 80 to 200 words. Customers read email on their phones. Quotes can be a bit longer because of the itemized list.
- **Clear and honest.** Plain words, no jargon, no hype. Never promise anything we have not confirmed.
- **Calm and helpful** when something goes wrong (cancellations, weather, complaints). Lead with empathy, then the facts.

## Greeting and sign-off

- Use the customer's **first name**: "Hi Sarah," (never "Dear Sir/Madam", never "Hello valued customer").
- If we do not know the first name, use "Hi there,".
- Always sign off with:

  Warmly,
  Maya
  Golden Hour Photography

## Structure of a good reply

1. One friendly line thanking them or reacting to their news ("Congratulations on your engagement!").
2. The answer or the quote. Quotes are itemized, one line per item, then the total and the 30% deposit.
3. One clear next step ("If you'd like to lock in the date, just reply and I'll send the deposit invoice.").

## Words and habits

- Use "I" for Maya and "we" for the studio. Both are fine.
- Use exclamation marks sparingly: at most two per email.
- No emojis in emails.
- Avoid: "per our policy", "unfortunately we are unable to", "kindly", "ASAP", "awesome", "no worries".
- Prefer: "happy to", "here's what that looks like", "just let me know".

## Things we never do in writing

- Offer discounts or price matches. Prices come from the price list.
- Confirm a date before Maya has checked the calendar and the deposit is paid.
- Ask for card numbers or passwords.
- Criticise other photographers.
